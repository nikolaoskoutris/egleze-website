About Egleze · Independent Podcast Intelligence

The best ideas of our time are buried in three-hour conversations.

Egleze is the desk that digs them out. We surface the most important moments from independent podcasts, every day — chosen by editors, not an algorithm built to keep you reacting.

Why we exist

The conversations that matter are the ones nobody has time for.

The most consequential thinking of this era happens in long-form conversation — hours with the scientist, the general, the diplomat, the doctor, the founder, the investigator, the person who was actually in the room. More gets worked out there in a week than on cable news in a month.

But almost nobody has three hours. So the best of it vanishes into the feed, and what survives isn't what's most worth hearing — it's what an algorithm decided would keep you reacting. We've optimised the information diet of a generation for engagement, not understanding.

Egleze exists to pull the signal back out: to find the moment that matters inside the long conversation, report who said it and where, and send you to the source so you can hear it whole.

What we do — and what we don't

We report that something was said. We don't certify that it's true.

This is the honest version of our job, because it's the question every serious reader should ask. When a former defence minister states on the record that an event was real, when a general makes a claim about a war, when a scientist describes a result — we bring you the moment, name the person, cite the show, and point you to the full conversation. What we vouch for is provenance: that this person said this, in this context, and that we've represented it faithfully.

We don't pretend to verify every claim, because no one honestly can. The voices in any conversation — on independent podcasts and in mainstream media alike — carry their own interests, agendas and blind spots. An analyst may be brilliant or may be funded. A general may be a witness or may be selling a line. Certifying the truth of all of it is beyond any newsroom, and outlets that imply otherwise are claiming a certainty they do not have.

So we don't make that claim. We give you the real words and the real source, and we trust you to judge. That is not us avoiding the work — it is a deliberate refusal to be the gatekeeper who decides for you what is true. That gatekeeping, performed badly for years, is a large part of why trust broke in the first place. We would rather hand you the primary source and respect your judgement than launder it through our own.

What earns a moment its place is not that we have proven it. It is that someone with real standing — who was in the room, did the work, held the post, took the risk — said it plainly, and it deserves to be weighed in public rather than lost.

The idea behind the name

Egleze is the modern assembly.

In ancient Athens, the ekklesia was the assembly where citizens gathered to hear, weigh and judge together — in common, in public, as equals. Egleze takes that one idea, and nothing more grand than it: a single surface where the full texture of serious conversation is held in common, and where plural people meet the same material and make up their own minds.

The breadth is deliberate — geopolitics and science, but also faith, money, history, the uncomfortable and the fringe. A space for judgement doesn't get to pick only the respectable voices.

What we built

Three things that make Egleze unlike your feed.

01

The Intelligence Bridge

We take the hours-long revelations from independent podcasts and report them with the clarity of a real news desk — human editorial judgement deciding what matters, attribution baked in, the source one click away. Long-form, made legible, without losing the signal that made it worth hearing.

Editorial judgement, not automation
02

The Five Reactions

We removed the like button. In its place, five honest responses — it inspired me, it concerned me, it made me curious, it changed how I think, it validated what I knew — each with intensity. There is deliberately no agree or disagree: we measure intellectual impact, not applause.

A spectrum, not a binary
03

Radical Privacy

Your reaction is yours alone. We show the room's heartbeat — the shared judgement of the community — but never the individual's hand. Remove the audience and you remove the performance. This is architecture, not a setting: not even we can see an individual's reaction.

Honesty, by design
What we are not

Defined as much by what we refuse to be.

Not an AI clip farm

Human editorial judgement is the product. What matters is decided by people who are accountable for it — not generated and forgotten.

Not engagement-maximising

The value is in the quality of attention you give a moment, not how long a system can keep you inside it.

Not another silo

Egleze is the connection point across audiences who never normally meet — not one more feed tuned to a single tribe.

Not Silicon Valley

Built in Europe, under the world's strongest data-protection norms, on purpose. Privacy-respecting by foundation, not retrofitted after the harm.

How we operate

Independence is not a slogan here. It is the structure.

Editorial independence

The Egleze desk decides what is surfaced and how it is framed. Podcasts and their creators are our sources, never our editors. No outside party — featured or not — directs our coverage.

A registered news organisation

Egleze Limited is registered in Ireland under the news-agency classification (NACE 6391), and operates with the responsibilities that designation carries.

Fair, attributed, brief

We use short, transformative excerpts for news reporting and commentary, always attributed, always pointing back to the original. We want you to go listen to the whole thing.

Correctable, accountable

Provenance is the thing we stand behind — so if we have misrepresented who said what, or taken a moment out of context, we correct it promptly. Getting the attribution right matters more than getting it first.

Privacy as a guarantee

Individual reactions are private by architecture. We see the totals; no one — including us — sees what any single person felt. It cannot be turned off, because it was never built to be.
